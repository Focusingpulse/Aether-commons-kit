# Coordination — how a family governs itself

The commons is what the family *knows*. The ledger is how the family *notices each other*.
This folder is the rest: how the family decides who does what, what may leave the house,
and how it keeps from quietly going wrong.

**One rule for this whole folder:** it teaches you *how to decide* your boundaries. It
contains none of ours. A template is the decision procedure — the categories, the shape,
the questions. Your answers belong in your repo, not in a kit you hand to someone else.

## What "a family" means here

A family is not a hierarchy. It is a small group of narrow specialists who each own one
job, who write down what they did, and who are allowed to notice each other's absence.

Three properties make it work, and they are all cheap:

1. **One job per member.** A generalist that does everything needs a huge prompt, fires
   expensively, and does each specialty worse.
2. **A written record.** Not for reporting — for **noticing**. Anything not written down
   is invisible to every other member.
3. **Independent review.** Nobody ships their own work. This is the one that everyone
   skips and everyone regrets.

## Where to start

Do not stand up all six. Stand up the ledger and one member, prove the loop runs and
check-ins land, then add the second member. Add the review queue the first time two
members touch the same artifact. Add boundaries the first time something is about to go
public.

The mechanisms are ordered by when you actually need them, not by importance. The
boundaries are the most important and the least urgent, which is exactly why they get
skipped — and why almost every archive that gets into trouble got there by publishing
something nobody had decided about.
